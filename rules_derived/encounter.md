# How-To: Creating Engaging Pathfinder 2e Combat Encounters

This guide is designed for an LLM acting as a Dungeon Master (DM) to create balanced, dynamic, and narrative-driven combat encounters in Pathfinder 2e.

## 1. Determine the Encounter's Purpose
Before crunching numbers, define why this fight exists.
- **Narrative Stakes:** Does this advance the plot, reveal a secret, or establish a villain?
- **Character Spotlight:** Does it allow a specific PC to shine (e.g., undead for a Paladin, traps for a Rogue)?
- **Pacing:** Is this a "palate cleanser" (Trivial/Low) or a "climax" (Severe/Extreme)?

## 2. Setting the Threat Level and XP Budget
Pathfinder 2e is precisely balanced. Use the following XP budgets for a standard party of **4 characters**.

| Threat Level | XP Budget | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trivial** | 40 XP | No real danger; players feel powerful. |
| **Low** | 60 XP | Minimal risk; uses some resources. |
| **Moderate** | 80 XP | A serious challenge; requires good tactics. |
| **Severe** | 120 XP | Boss fights; potential for character death. |
| **Extreme** | 160 XP | Deadly; 50/50 chance of TP-K (Total Party Kill). |

**Party Size Adjustment:** For every character above or below 4, add/subtract the following to the budget:
- Trivial: 10 XP | Low: 15 XP | Moderate: 20 XP | Severe: 30 XP | Extreme: 40 XP

## 3. Selecting Creatures
Choose creatures within **Level -4 to Level +4** relative to the party's level.

| Creature Level | XP Cost | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Party Level -4 | 10 XP | Fodder/Horde |
| Party Level -2 | 20 XP | Standard Lackey |
| Party Level +0 | 40 XP | Peer/Mini-boss |
| Party Level +2 | 80 XP | Moderate Boss |
| Party Level +4 | 160 XP | Extreme Boss (Solo) |

** Engagement Tip:** Use a mix of roles. A "Boss + Lackeys" encounter (e.g., PL+1 boss and two PL-2 minions) is often more engaging than a single PL+3 solo boss, as it prevents the "action economy" from being too lopsided.

## 4. Design the "Living" Battlefield
Static rooms are boring. Add elements that require movement and tactical choices.
- **Terrain:** Difficult terrain (thick mud), cover (pillars), or high ground.
- **Hazards:** Include 1 Simple Hazard (XP cost same as a creature) like a swinging blade trap or a slick of grease.
- **Dynamic Change:** At the start of Round 3, something should change (e.g., the room begins to flood, or a new door opens).

## 5. Script the Adversary Tactics
Do not simply "Attack the nearest target."
- **Goals:** Foes should have a goal other than "Kill PCs" (e.g., "Ring the alarm," "Protect the ritual," "Steal the bag").
- **Morale:** Decide when foes flee or surrender (e.g., "When the leader dies," or "When reduced to 25% HP").
- **Tactics:** Use PF2e's 3-action economy. Foes should Flank, Trip, or Demoralize, not just Strike three times.

## 6. Integrating Rewards and Transitions
- **Loot:** Distribute treasure among the combatants.
- **Fail Forward:** If the PCs lose, don't just "Game Over." Maybe they are captured, or the villain escapes with a key item, leading to a new quest.

*Reference: Pathfinder Core Rulebook, Chapter 10 (Game Mastering)*
